# Reference boards

Public-domain pictures and the studio's own, with what each one teaches and its licence. Nothing from a living artist
or another game is ever copied in here: those are looked at and written about, not kept.

## The two look boards: which kind of dark, which kind of bright (Leonard, AD, 2026-11-20)

Made to ask Robin open question 20 of the brief while the theme was open: "Dark fantasy" (Robin, 2026-11-19), then
"Maybe vibrant pirate is a better theme" (Robin, 2026-11-20). Two boards of nine old paintings and prints, numbered 1
to 18, so he can say "more like 10 and 16, never 13". They show kinds of look, not what Drok will look like.

Later the same day he picked the theme: "Vibrant pirate: bright seas, green islands, colourful crews and beasts.
Nothing of dark fantasy comes back." (Robin, 2026-11-20, his pick, as Victor, GD reported it.) So **the dark nine are
set aside** as a mood. They stay here because the way a picture is drawn (an ink line, flat colour, solid paint) can
still be pointed at. The bright nine are the live ones.

- The boards as pictures: `dark-fantasy\_board.jpg` (1 to 9) and `bright-sea\_board.jpg` (10 to 18), made with
  `tools\numbered.js`.
- Both as one page for his phone: `dark-fantasy\kinds-of-dark.html`, published as a private artifact,
  https://claude.ai/artifact/RNP43qsmnP3Pwy1DLNGVYP. The published page has all eighteen pictures beside it; on disk the
  bright nine sit in `bright-sea\`, so the local file shows only the first nine.
- Where each file came from: `_fetched.json` in each folder (the Commons page, the artist, the date, the licence).

All of these are from Wikimedia Commons, each marked **Public domain** or **CC0** there (checked per file by
`tools\commons_fetch.py` on 2026-11-20, which refuses anything else). Kept at 800 px wide, about 5 MB for the three
boards together.

## pirate-world: what the studio studies (Leonard, AD, 2026-11-20)

Not a question for Robin: the things a pirate world is made of, to learn from before the first stills. Numbered 19 to
27 so he can point at one if it jumps out. The board is `pirate-world\_board.jpg`; the records are in its
`_fetched.json`.

| No. | File | The picture | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 19 | `19-pyle-kidd-on-deck.jpg` | Howard Pyle, "Kidd on the Deck of the Adventure Galley", before 1911 | A captain's shape: wide hat, long coat, a red sash. Grey everywhere, so the red is the thing that burns. |
| 20 | `20-pyle-buccaneer.jpg` | Howard Pyle, "The Buccaneer was a Picturesque Fellow", 1905 | A fighter's shape: cloak, sash, a long gun, bare sand. The story-book pirate everyone knows. |
| 21 | `21-audubon-flamingo.jpg` | Robert Havell after John James Audubon, "American Flamingo", 1838 (CC0) | A beast that is one hot colour and one strong shape. Reads from across a room. |
| 22 | `22-haeckel-octopuses.jpg` | Ernst Haeckel, "Kunstformen der Natur", plate 54, 1904 | Sea beasts as they are: every one a different outline. Orange and yellow on pale paper. |
| 23 | `23-haeckel-crabs.jpg` | Ernst Haeckel, "Kunstformen der Natur", plate 86, 1904 | Crabs and lobsters seen from above, which is our camera. Ten body plans on one sheet. |
| 24 | `24-dutch-sea-chart-west-indies.jpg` | A Dutch sea chart of the West Indies, 1672 | A working chart: compass lines across pale sea, islands as small coloured shapes. The chart screen. |
| 25 | `25-boazio-santo-domingo.jpg` | Baptista Boazio, Drake at Santo Domingo, 1589 | A harbour town and its bay from above, with ships and sea beasts drawn in. Green, red and gold. |
| 26 | `26-blaeu-west-indies-map.jpg` | Willem Blaeu, "Insulae Americanae", about 1640 | A printed map of the islands with a title piece, ships and compass roses. How a chart is dressed. |
| 27 | `27-oliva-portolan-chart.jpg` | Joan Oliva, a portolan chart, 1594 | A sailor's chart on a whole skin: wind roses, red and gold marks, the shape of the hide itself. |
